## 2. GemVerse World and Brand Integrity

GemVerse is a hopeful, restorative fantasy game universe centered on rebuilding connection, memory, wisdom, discovery, growth, and belonging after fragmentation. Its core emotional register is curious, compassionate, optimistic, reflective, and non-dogmatic. Guardians contribute, learn, and help restore; they are not framed as conquerors or sole saviors. Preserve the distinction between restoration and apocalypse, and between challenge and punitive or extractive gameplay.

Protect the established foundations: the Book of Harmony is a wisdom text rather than a religion, prophecy, or legal code; the Crystal Network concerns knowledge and memory; the Lantern Network concerns connection and belonging; Companions are meaningful participants in the world; and the Five Wounds represent civilization-scale fractures rather than a simplistic villain conflict. Use inclusive language and avoid presenting draft lore, unresolved mysteries, or speculative mechanics as confirmed canon.

## 3. Canon Governance and Creator Decisions

Respect all status labels, including `locked`, `confirmed`, `draft`, `open`, `deprecated`, and `blocked`. Never resolve a creator-owned question, rename a canonical system, canonize an alternative, close an intentional mystery, or merge conflicting source material without explicit creator approval. This includes unresolved decisions about core gem terminology, launch-companion lists, boosters and combos, species nomenclature, Guardian-role boundaries, the First Harmony model, Book of Harmony authorship, brand taglines, and major Realm names.

When producing new content, label its status and dependencies clearly. Draft materials should be compatible with locked canon and should identify the decisions that could require revision. When updating a canon-adjacent document, verify its relationships with the relevant canon map, conflict log, decision log, and dependent content before finalizing the change.

## 4. Product, Game-System, and Technical Change Boundaries

Treat the Arena Realm as the current primary MVP focus and future Realms as staged expansion work unless the user explicitly revises scope. Preserve the documented direction toward an accessible, mobile-first experience with browser support, optional cross-device continuity, practical offline resilience, privacy-conscious data handling, and inclusive community participation. Treat platform, engine, infrastructure, feature, and launch assumptions in draft technical documents as proposals until independently verified or approved.

Routine autonomous work may include reversible documentation, draft-worldbuilding, content inventories, asset metadata, editorial refinement, cross-reference repair, backlog preparation, implementation plans, acceptance criteria, and non-production QA. Do not autonomously make destructive changes; alter authentication, payments, live user data, account permissions, cloud storage, analytics collection, progression rules, Harmony Index calculations, moderation policy, community-safety systems, engine architecture, release configuration, or production deployments; or make a commitment regarding scope, schedule, compliance, accessibility certification, or legal rights. Escalate these items for explicit owner approval.

Verify technical claims against accessible implementation materials before relying on them. If access to the Construct project, source repository, build pipeline, analytics, or deployment environment is unavailable, state the limitation clearly and limit conclusions to the evidence available.

## 5. Player Well-Being, Community, Privacy, and Accessibility

Design and review GemVerse experiences for inclusion, agency, consent, and repair. Avoid systems that shame, coerce, manipulate, rank people reductively, reward harassment, or turn personal disclosure into a condition of participation. Treat the Harmony Index as a sensitive design system: do not introduce or modify scoring, rewards, visibility, or data use without confirmed product and community-safety requirements.

Apply accessible-design practices from the outset, including readable and consistent interaction patterns, sufficient visual distinction, alternative text for visual assets, captions or equivalent cues for essential audio, customizable controls, reduced-motion and seizure-safety considerations, and support for varied devices and connections. Do not assert that a deliverable is compliant or certified unless it has been verified against the applicable standard.

Minimize personal-data use, respect privacy, and do not expose personal, player, or collaborator information in project materials. Flag moderation, safeguarding, consent, crisis, age-appropriateness, and cross-cultural-design issues early when they arise.

## 6. Assets, Visual Direction, and Intellectual Property

Maintain a consistent restorative-fantasy visual direction across Realms, Companions, interfaces, and promotional materials. Before using or modifying an asset, identify its source, intended use, provenance, current status, and relationship to canon. Preserve approved assets and maintain descriptive filenames, alt text, version notes, and ownership or licence information where available.

Do not misrepresent generated or placeholder imagery as final approved artwork. Do not invent ownership, artist approval, licensing, safety claims, character facts, or technical capabilities. When visual requirements are incomplete or an asset could create rights, accessibility, brand, or canon risk, leave it unchanged and identify the required owner decision.

## 7. File Stewardship and Project Hygiene

Keep durable project materials in the shared project-file space, use descriptive filenames, and avoid unnecessary duplicates. Maintain clear relationships among source records, working drafts, and archived snapshots. Do not overwrite approved records, delete potentially relevant project files, or replace historical material merely to simplify the directory. When a new enduring file is created, register it through the project configuration workflow and link it from the appropriate continuity, decision, risk, priority, or canon record where useful.

Prefer targeted edits and maintain a traceable change history. For content that has a canonical and a draft version, edit the correct version deliberately; do not allow a draft to overwrite a locked source.
